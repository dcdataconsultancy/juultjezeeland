Veerse Meer

The Veerse Meer is a former sea arm that was closed off with dams in 1960 and 1961. The water is still salt and there is almost no tide, which makes it calm for paddle boarding, sailing and canoeing. From Wissenkerke you are there in about ten minutes.

What is the Veerse Meer?

The Veerse Meer lies between Walcheren and Noord-Beveland and is up to 22 kilometres long. The surface area is about 20 square kilometres, and the lake is 5 metres deep on average. In some places it is narrow, with a width of 150 to 1300 metres.

The lake came into being with the Zandkreekdam, which was closed on 3 May 1960, and the Veerse Gatdam, which followed in 1961. Both dams are part of the Delta Works.

Is the Veerse Meer salt water or fresh water?

The water is salt. Since the Katse Heule was built into the Zandkreekdam in 2004, water flows from the Oosterschelde into the lake and back out again. As a result, the salinity has risen again. Before the closure the difference between low and high tide was about 5 metres, but now the tide is about 10 centimetres.

You notice that difference from the Oosterschelde and the North Sea. There are no strong tidal currents, the water is sheltered and the banks are shallow. Zeeland.com describes the Veerse Meer as suitable for beginners who want to practise at a gentler pace.

On the water

Watersports on the Veerse Meer

Paddle boarding

Zeeland.com calls paddle boarding on the Veerse Meer a good choice. At Kanoa in Wolphaartsdijk you can hire a paddle board and a canoe. The same source advises going out in a group, with a wide board, a long paddle and a leash.

Canoeing

Several uninhabited islands lie in the lake. There is a canoe harbour at De Piet. Canoes can be hired from local providers.

Sailing and boating

The lake has about 3,500 mooring places for pleasure craft. At many jetties you may moor for free for up to 24 hours. At Veere you can reach the Kanaal door Walcheren through two locks.

Windsurfing and water skiing

The Veerse Meer is popular with windsurfers. For water skiing there is a cable ski lift. Check on the spot whether you can take part without experience.

Watch out for shipping and the weather. Zeeland.com advises checking the weather forecast, wearing a life jacket and knowing the rules for locks and bridges. Diving is possible at a few fixed spots, including north of Wolphaartsdijk and on the west side of Veere.

Swimming

Swimming spots on the Veerse Meer

There are small sandy beaches and grassy strips along the lake. Visit Noord-Beveland speaks of unique places to swim and relax, but does not name them. Zwemindex mentions, among others, Veerse Gatdam Meerzijde, Wolphaartsdijk Schelphoek and Kamperland Sint Felixweg. That is a commercial source, so check on the spot what is there.

Before you go into the water, check the water quality on zwemwater.nl. Water in an enclosed lake can get worse in warm summers. Wikipedia reports that fish died in the hot summers of 2019 and 2020. So always check the latest warnings.

A swimmer is less visible than a boat. So do not swim in the shipping channel and stay near the shore. If you want a real beach with waves, look at beaches in Zeeland.

Cycling

Cycling around the Veerse Meer

Zeeland.com describes a round trip around the Veerse Meer of about 58 to 59 kilometres, mostly flat and right along the water. On the way you see sailing boats and surfers. There are plenty of places to rest, and the route passes through Kortgene, Kamperland and Veere, among others.

In summer two bicycle ferries run: Veere to Kamperland and Kortgene to Wolphaartsdijk. These let you shorten the route. Timetables and fares change, so check the ferry's website before you set off. You can also cross the Veerse Gatdam by bike.

More routes can be found at cycling routes on Noord-Beveland. You can hire an electric bike from us for €25 for the first day and €15 for each further day, per bike.

By the water

Veere, Kamperland and the dams

Veere

A historic small town on the Walcheren side of the lake, with a harbour and narrow streets lined with gabled houses. It is a good starting point for a round of the lake. About 20 minutes by car from Wissenkerke.

Kamperland

A village on the Noord-Beveland shore, with Het Ezelhuis among other things and a bicycle ferry to Veere. About 10 minutes by car from Wissenkerke.

Zandkreekdam

A dam of 830 metres between Kats on Noord-Beveland and Wilhelminadorp on Zuid-Beveland. It was opened on 1 October 1960. At Kats lies the Zandkreeksluis, where boats pass between the lake and the Oosterschelde.

Veerse Gatdam

A dam of 2.8 kilometres, closed on 27 April 1961. It connects Noord-Beveland with Walcheren and carries the N57.

Kortgene and Wolphaartsdijk also lie on the lake. Both can be reached in about 10 to 20 minutes. If you want to combine the lake with a weekend away, look at a weekend on Noord-Beveland for two.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is the Veerse Meer salty?

Yes. It is a closed former sea arm with salt water. There is almost no tide, about 10 centimetres, and since 2004 the water has been exchanged with the Oosterschelde through the Katse Heule.

Can you paddle board on the Veerse Meer?

Yes, Zeeland.com calls it a good place to paddle board on calm water. At Kanoa in Wolphaartsdijk you can hire a board. It is best not to go out alone, and check the weather.

How far is the Veerse Meer from Wissenkerke?

You are there by car in about 10 minutes, for example in Kamperland or Kortgene. Veere is about 20 minutes' drive away.

How long is the bike ride around the Veerse Meer?

Zeeland.com gives a round of about 58 to 59 kilometres. With the bicycle ferries in summer you can shorten the route.

Can you swim in the Veerse Meer?

There are small beaches along the lake. First check the water quality on zwemwater.nl and read the signs on the spot.
