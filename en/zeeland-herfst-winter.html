Zeeland

Zeeland in autumn and winter

Out of season Zeeland is quiet, with empty beaches, lots of birds and sometimes strong wind. A weekend in Zeeland in autumn or winter is about walking, watching storms and birds. Not everything is open then, so check in advance what you want to visit.

Why visit Zeeland out of season?

It is quieter. On beaches, dykes and cycle paths you meet far fewer people than in summer.

Accommodation is cheaper. At Juultje Zeeland you pay from €86 per night for the holiday cottage and from €72 per night for the apartment in the low season. In the high season that is from €130 and €100. You will see which dates fall in the low season when you book.

The weather is changeable. That is exactly why many people come: a brisk walk along the dyke, then warming up indoors.

Outdoors

What you can do outdoors

Beach walks

The beach is 2.5 kilometres from the accommodations. In autumn and winter you often have much of it to yourself. Bring windproof clothing.

Watching storms

The Oosterscheldekering closes when a forecast water level of 3 metres above NAP is reached at Roompot Buiten. Closing takes about 75 minutes. Since 1986 it has been closed 27 times, never as a test. In strong wind you can therefore climb onto a dyke to watch.

Birdwatching

In winter there are many more waterbirds in the Delta. You can read more about this further down this page.

Cycling

An electric bike helps against the wind. At Juultje it costs €25 per bike for the first day and €15 per day after that. You will find routes at cycling routes on Noord-Beveland.

Dog

With a dog on the beach in autumn and winter

Noord-Beveland municipality says a dog may run free outside built up areas, unless signs say otherwise. Within built up areas your dog must be on a lead. There are no official off lead zones.

Zeeland.com reports that signs on every beach show where and when dogs may run free. In high season stricter rules often apply, so outside summer there is more room. This differs from beach to beach.

At Banjaardstrand dogs must be on a lead from 1 April to 1 October. We have not found what applies outside that period, so read the signs on the spot and keep your dog on a lead if it does not come straight away when you call.

Always pick up the dog mess, including on the beach. More rules are at dog beaches on Noord-Beveland and holiday with your dog on Noord-Beveland.

Birds

Geese and waders

Rijkswaterstaat reports that from November to February there are about five times as many birds in the Delta as in summer. That concerns about 500,000 waterbirds in winter compared with about 100,000 in summer (as of March 2025).

They rest on sandbanks that stay dry at high water and feed on the mudflats at low water. Some areas are closed to limit disturbance. Please keep to this.

Spoonbills increasingly overwinter in the Delta, more than a hundred. The little egret is increasing strongly. The oystercatcher, on the other hand, is declining: about 15,000 now, compared with about 50,000 thirty years ago.

Geese such as greylag, white fronted, barnacle and brent geese migrate to Zeeland's polders in autumn, winter and spring. Sovon counts them from October to March inclusive. Binoculars are enough, as you can see many geese from the dyke.

We have not been able to confirm specific viewing spots on Noord-Beveland. Ask on the spot, or walk a stretch of dyke along the Oosterschelde.

Would you also like to see animals on the water? Then read seal spotting in the Oosterschelde. Keep in mind that the boat trip at Neeltje Jans may be cancelled because of storms between October and April.

Food

Mussels in autumn and winter

The Zeeland mussel season usually starts at the beginning of July. The exact date differs from year to year. We have not been able to confirm until when you can get fresh Zeeland mussels.

So ask at a fish restaurant or fish shop nearby before you go. More about mussels, oysters and other regional products can be found at Zeeland specialities.

Indoors

What to check

Many attractions and terraces have different opening hours outside the season, or are closed. This varies by place and by year. Always check the attraction's website.

We do not list winter events. On 10 November 2026 the events page of Visit Noord-Beveland said that no events had been found. So look up current event calendars yourself.

For a dry day in bad weather you will find day trips for rainy weather in Zeeland, such as the Watersnoodmuseum. If you want to see the dams and barriers, read Visiting the Delta Works.

More ideas can be found in the overview of outings in Zeeland.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is Zeeland worth visiting in winter?

Yes, if you are looking for peace, walks and birds. Expect wind and changeable weather, and check the opening hours of attractions in advance.

Can your dog run free on the beach in winter?

That depends on the signs on the beach. Outside built up areas a dog may run free on Noord-Beveland unless signs say otherwise. Keeping it on a lead is sensible if it does not come straight away when you call.

Which birds can you see in Zeeland in winter?

Among others, geese, spoonbills, little egrets and oystercatchers. In winter there are about 500,000 waterbirds in the Delta.

Are accommodations cheaper in winter?

In the low season the holiday cottage costs from €86 per night and the apartment from €72 per night. Check availability for the exact price.
