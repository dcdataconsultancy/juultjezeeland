Zeeland

Zeeland specialities

The best known regional products are Zeeland mussels and Oosterschelde oysters from Yerseke, followed by Zeeland bolus, babbelaars and the salty sea vegetables sea lavender and marsh samphire. Mussels are available from around July. Below, you can read per product what it is and where you can generally taste or buy it.

Zeeland mussels: when is the season?

Zeeland mussels are usually in season from around early July. In 2024 the season officially began on 3 July. The start date varies by year: in 2018 the mussels ripened four weeks later than in the years before. According to a consumer guide, the season runs until about April.

Mussels are farmed nowadays, no longer caught wild. At the end of the nineteenth century Yerseke became the centre of modern mussel farming. The number of mussels in a year can vary. In 2024 growers expected fewer mussels due to lots of rain and foam algae. So ask about what is available on the spot.

The classic preparation is a brief cooking with finely chopped onion and parsley, a little butter added, with white wine or local beer. Whether there is a Mosseldag this year, and when, we cannot confirm. Check that with the tourist office of Yerseke.

Taste

Zeeland products in brief

Oosterschelde oysters

There are two types: the Japanese oyster, raw or grilled, and the flat oyster, almost always raw with pepper and lemon. In the centre of Yerseke there are still historic oyster beds from the nineteenth century that are in use. The Oosterschelde Museum tells the story of the oyster and the mussel, and there are boat tours to the mussel plots.

Zeeuwse bolus

A spiral of white bread dough rolled with dark brown sugar and baked. Almost every Zeeland baker makes it themselves. The bolus has Jewish origins and came to Zeeland at the end of the sixteenth century. You often get butter with it.

Boterbabbelaars

A sweet made from butter, sugar, glucose syrup, vinegar and water. The name means little chatterbox, because you suck on it for a long time. You find them at village festivals and in souvenir shops, and they are eaten with coffee or tea. A baker from Middelburg started making them in 1892.

Sea lavender and glasswort

Sea lavender grows on the higher parts of the mudflats and looks like lamb's lettuce. The harvest runs from mid March to the end of July. Glasswort grows on the bare mudflats and takes up a lot of salt. The harvest runs from mid May to mid September.

Zeeuws spek

Zeeuws spek is a regional product made from pork belly. It is not smoked but brined, and the slices are thicker than ordinary bacon. You eat it on bread, with stamppot or pea soup, or from the barbecue. Shop versions are often seasoned or partly cooked.

Oysters in Yerseke: what is there to do?

Yerseke is the village where mussels and oysters come together. Next to the oyster beds there are cafés and restaurants where you can eat mussels and oysters. If you want to know the story behind it, visit the Oosterschelde Museum. There are also boat trips to the mussel beds. Check the current times and prices with the providers yourself.

We have not found an oyster season with exact dates. Ask on the spot or at the tourist office.

Where can you taste or buy Zeeland specialities?

Mussels and oysters are best eaten on the spot in Yerseke, at a fish restaurant or a fish shop. Outside Yerseke you will find mussel restaurants in Philippine and a large mussel sculpture in Bruinisse, but check on the spot that they serve food.

You can buy bolussen and babbelaars at the bakery and in regional shops. Many Zeeland products carry the Zeker Zeeuws quality mark: the product comes from Zeeland, is processed here and is made sustainably. Glasswort and sea lavender are seasonal vegetables, so in season check restaurant menus or regional producers.

If you are in one of the small towns, you can combine tasting with your visit there, see Zeeland towns for a day out.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

When are Zeeland mussels in season?

From about the beginning of July until about April. The official start varies from year to year.

Where do you eat oysters in Yerseke?

In the centre of Yerseke, by the oyster beds, there are cafés and restaurants where you can eat mussels and oysters.

What is a Zeeuwse bolus?

A baked spiral of bread dough with dark brown sugar. You buy it from Zeeland bakers and it often comes with butter.

What is sea lavender?

A salty sea vegetable that grows on the higher mudflats and looks like lamb's lettuce. The harvest runs from mid March to the end of July.
